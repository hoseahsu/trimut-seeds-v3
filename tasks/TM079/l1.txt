A gift buyer selects recordings from different catalog categories.

Mira's birthday is next week and Nolan has decided to buy a present for her. He knows she loves recordings so he goes to the local record shop, where there are n recordings on sale from one of m categories.

In the record shop, Nolan decides to buy two recordings of different categories.

Based on the category of recordings on sale in the shop, find the number of options available to Nolan for choosing two recordings of different categories for Mira. Options are considered different if they differ in at least one recording.

The recordings are given by indices of their categories. The categories are numbered from 1 to m.

Input

The first line contains two positive integers n and m (2 ≤ n ≤ 2·10^5, 2 ≤ m ≤ 10) — the number of recordings in the bookstore and the number of categories.

The second line contains a sequence a1, a2, ..., an, where ai (1 ≤ ai ≤ m) equals the category of the i-th recording.

It is guaranteed that for each category there is at least one recording of that category.

Output

Print the only integer — the number of ways in which Nolan can choose recordings.

It is guaranteed that the answer doesn't exceed the value 2·10^9.

Examples

Input

4 3
2 1 3 1


Output

5


Input

7 4
4 2 3 1 2 4 3


Output

18

Note

The answer to the first test sample equals 5 as Sasha can choose:

  1. the first and second recordings, 
  2. the first and third recordings, 
  3. the first and fourth recordings, 
  4. the second and third recordings, 
  5. the third and fourth recordings.
