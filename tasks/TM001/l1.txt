# Instructor Mira

An instructor selects assembly puzzles as awards for apprentices.


The end of the academy year is near and Instructor Mira, the instructor, will soon have to say goodbye to a yet another training group. She decided to prepare a goodbye award for her n apprentices and give each of them a jigsaw puzzle (which, as wikipedia states, is a tiling puzzle that requires the assembly of numerous small, often oddly shaped, interlocking and tessellating pieces).

The shop assistant told the instructor that there are m puzzles in the shop, but they might differ in difficulty and size. Specifically, the first jigsaw puzzle consists of f_1 pieces, the second one consists of f_2 pieces and so on.

Instructor Mira doesn't want to upset the apprentices, so she decided that the difference between the numbers of pieces in her awards must be as small as possible. Let A be the number of pieces in the largest puzzle that the instructor buys and B be the number of pieces in the smallest such puzzle. She wants to choose such n puzzles that A - B is minimum possible. Help the instructor and find the least possible value of A - B.

Input

The first line contains space-separated integers n and m (2 ≤ n ≤ m ≤ 50). The second line contains m space-separated integers f_1, f_2, ..., f_m (4 ≤ f_i ≤ 1000) — the quantities of pieces in the puzzles sold in the shop.

Output

Print a single integer — the least possible difference the instructor can obtain.

Examples

Input

4 6
10 12 10 7 5 22

Output

5

Note

Sample 1. The training group has 4 apprentices. The shop sells 6 puzzles. If Instructor Mira buys the first four puzzles consisting of 10, 12, 10 and 7 pieces correspondingly, then the difference between the sizes of the largest and the smallest puzzle will be equal to 5. It is impossible to obtain a smaller difference. Note that the instructor can also buy puzzles 1, 3, 4 and 5 to obtain the difference 5.
