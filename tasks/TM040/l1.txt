A market accepts denomination vouchers and gives voucher change.

In the Territory of Arden, only vouchers are used as currency. There are 10^{100}+1 kinds of vouchers, with the values of 1, 10, 10^2, 10^3, \dots, 10^{(10^{100})}. You have come shopping at a market and are now buying a snack press with a value of N. (The snack is the name of a Japanese snack.)

To make the payment, you will choose some amount of money which is at least N and give it to the cashier. Then, the cashier gives you back the change, which is the amount of money you give minus N.

What will be the minimum possible number of total vouchers used by you and the cashier, when both choose the combination of vouchers to minimize this count?

Assume that you have sufficient numbers of vouchers, and so does the cashier.

Constraints

* N is an integer between 1 and 10^{1,000,000} (inclusive).

Input

Input is given from Standard Input in the following format:


N


Output

Print the minimum possible number of total vouchers used by you and the cashier.

Examples

Input

36


Output

8


Input

91


Output

3


Input

314159265358979323846264338327950288419716939937551058209749445923078164062862089986280348253421170


Output

243
