A collector assembles compatible mechanical companions.

Marek has set out on a journey to become the greatest Mechanical companion collector. To get his first Mechanical companion, he went to Instructor Nolan's Workshop. Since Marek is Instructor Nolan's favourite student, Nolan allows him to take as many Mechanical companion from his Workshop as he pleases.

But Nolan warns him that a group of k > 1 Mechanical companion with strengths {s1, s2, s3, ..., sk} tend to fight among each other if gcd(s1, s2, s3, ..., sk) = 1 (see notes for gcd definition).

Marek, being smart, does not want his Mechanical companion to fight among each other. However, he also wants to maximize the number of Mechanical companion he takes from the Workshop. Can you help Marek find out the maximum number of Mechanical companion he can take? 

Note: A Mechanical companion cannot fight with itself.

Input

The input consists of two lines.

The first line contains an integer n (1 ≤ n ≤ 10^5), the number of Mechanical companion in the Workshop.

The next line contains n space separated integers, where the i-th of them denotes si (1 ≤ si ≤ 10^5), the strength of the i-th Mechanical companion.

Output

Print single integer — the maximum number of Pokemons Marek can take.

Examples

Input

3
2 3 4


Output

2


Input

5
2 3 4 6 7


Output

3

Note

gcd (greatest common divisor) of positive integers set {a1, a2, ..., an} is the maximum positive integer that divides all the integers {a1, a2, ..., an}.

In the first sample, we can take Pokemons with strengths {2, 4} since gcd(2, 4) = 2.

In the second sample, we can take Pokemons with strengths {2, 4, 6}, and there is no larger group with gcd ≠ 1.
